## Why this matters

Natural origin does not mean risk-free. Dermatologists document **allergic contact dermatitis** and irritation from tea tree, lavender, peppermint, and other oils—especially undiluted or after repeated exposure.

## What researchers did

Reviews and patch-test series analyze patients with eczema or suspected fragrance allergy, testing standardized essential oil allergens. Phototoxicity studies measure **furocoumarin** reactions after citrus oil application and UV exposure.

## Main findings

- Essential oils are among the **more frequent botanical allergen sources** in specialty clinic series when used topically.
- **Bergamot** and some **cold-pressed citrus** oils contain phototoxic compounds; UV exposure after skin application can cause burns or pigmentation changes.
- **Sensitization rates** vary by population; occupational handlers (massage therapists, hairdressers) see more cases.
- Inhalation at moderate home diffusion is less studied for long-term respiratory effects; people with asthma may react to strong scents.

## What this does not prove

Patch-test data do not mean everyone will react—but they support **dilution, patch testing on a small skin area, and avoiding sun** after phototoxic oils on skin.

## Practical context

When using [lemon](/book/lemon) or [bergamot](/book/bergamot) in skin blends, follow dilution guidance and sun avoidance; prefer steam-distilled citrus when phototoxicity is a concern.

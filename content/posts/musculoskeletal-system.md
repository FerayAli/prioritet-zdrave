## How does the system work?

Bones are the frame and a mineral bank. Joints are where the frame moves. Muscles and tendons pull across those joints. Ligaments limit the range. Nerves tell the muscles when to fire, which is why a “tight” back is often coordination as much as muscle. Ordinary stiffness from sitting is in scope here.

Pain after a fall, a joint that locks or gives way, numbness, weakness, or back pain with bladder or bowel changes needs care. This page is not a rehab plan.

## Emotions and psychosomatics

Pain is louder after a short night and during a frightened or angry week. Guarding — bracing a sore spot — is a nervous-system habit that can outlast the original strain. That does not mean the pain is invented. It means mood, sleep, and attention change how much the same joint occupies the day. Stretching away a serious injury because you have decided it is “just stress” is the mistake to avoid.

## Aromatherapy

Frankincense research on inflammation usually concerns Boswellia extracts taken by mouth, not the essential oil in a carrier. Those are different materials: [frankincense versus boswellia extracts](/blog/frankincense-boswellia-inflammation). A rub can make a stretch pleasanter. It does not rebuild cartilage.

**Stiff-shoulder comfort** — diluted on skin

- Lavender — 2 drops
- Stir into 1 teaspoon of a plain carrier oil
- Massage a stiff, intact area of muscle. Skip broken skin, the eyes, and any spot that is hot, swollen, or worsening
- Stop at redness or burning. This is not a pain medicine.

## Daily care

- Leave sitting every half hour or so, even for a minute.
- Keep one or two strength habits you will repeat: a sit-to-stand, a carried bag, a slow squat to an honest depth.
- Match a new walk or class to last month, not to an ideal week.
- Sleep is when ordinary soreness settles. A late, short night makes the same desk feel harsher.
- Eat enough to support the training you are actually doing. A warm shower is a reasonable comfort for stiffness.

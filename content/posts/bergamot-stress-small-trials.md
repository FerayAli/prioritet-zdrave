## Why this matters

Bergamot’s citrus-floral scent appears in stress and mood research, but the human trial base is **smaller** than for lavender.

## What researchers did

Randomized and controlled studies have exposed healthy volunteers or outpatient groups to **bergamot essential oil inhalation** (often via diffusion or cotton balls) for short sessions, measuring self-reported mood, blood pressure, or salivary cortisol compared with rest or control aromas.

## Main findings

- Some trials report **lower cortisol** or improved mood/anxiety scores after bergamot inhalation versus control, particularly in settings designed to induce mild stress.
- Effects are often **short-term** (single session to a few weeks).
- Blinding is difficult with scented interventions; placebo control design varies.
- Results do not consistently show large changes in all measured vitals.

## What this does not prove

Bergamot essential oil is **phototoxic** when applied to skin before sun exposure (see our safety post). Inhalation studies do not justify ingesting oil. Findings in healthy students or workers may not generalize to clinical anxiety disorders.

## Why this matters

Many people reach for lavender at bedtime. Sleep outcomes are harder to measure than anxiety questionnaires, so reviews matter for setting expectations.

## What researchers did

Systematic reviews of lavender interventions (often inhalation or aromatherapy massage) in adults, including hospitalized patients, perimenopausal women, and people with self-reported poor sleep. Designs vary from single-night lab studies to multi-week programs.

## Main findings

- Pooled analyses that include **lavender inhalation** often report improvements in **subjective sleep quality** and related anxiety scores.
- Some trials in specific settings (e.g. coronary care, midlife sleep complaints) reported better sleep scores with aromatherapy protocols using lavender compared with control conditions.
- **Objective polysomnography** improvements are less consistently reported than questionnaire gains.
- Effect sizes depend on **session length, concentration, and whether lavender was combined** with other oils or nursing care rituals.

## What this does not prove

A few deep breaths over a diffuser may not replicate hospital protocols. Chronic insomnia may need clinical evaluation. Lavender is not a substitute for sleep hygiene or prescribed sleep medicine.

## Practical context

Evening routines on this site sometimes mention lavender as a familiar scent cue—not as a guaranteed sleep drug. Read [lavender](/book/lavender) for our educational description.

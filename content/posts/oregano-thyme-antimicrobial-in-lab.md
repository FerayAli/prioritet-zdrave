## Why this matters

Oregano and thyme essential oils are rich in **phenolic monoterpenes** (carvacrol, thymol) that score highly in antimicrobial susceptibility tests—fuel for both research interest and overstated marketing.

## What researchers did

Narrative and systematic reviews compile in vitro and animal data on essential oils against bacteria, fungi, and viruses, with fewer large human RCTs for whole oils. Studies specify chemotype, MIC values, and sometimes vapor-phase exposure.

## Main findings

- Reviews report **broad in vitro activity** for oregano/thyme oils and isolated carvacrol/thymol against food-spoilage and clinically relevant microbes at micromolar to percent concentrations.
- Human evidence for **ingested** oregano oil exists in small trials for specific indications but is not interchangeable with aromatherapy.
- **Mucosal irritation** and drug interactions are concerns at high internal doses.

## What this does not prove

Killing bacteria in a plate does not mean diffusing oregano oil prevents illness. Concentrated phenolic oils can burn skin and airways if undiluted.

## Practical context

[Oregano](/book/oregano) and [thyme](/book/thyme) pages describe culinary-herb familiarity—we stress dilution and avoiding internal use without professional guidance.

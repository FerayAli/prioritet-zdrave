## How does the system work?

The lymphatic system is a slow network of vessels and nodes. It picks up fluid that leaves the bloodstream, checks it for unfamiliar proteins, and returns the fluid. Nodes in the neck, armpits, and groin are places where immune cells meet what the body has seen. You are not meant to feel lymph “working” every day. Swelling that stays, redness with fever, or a firm node that does not settle needs a clinician — not massage oil.

“Lymphatic drainage” as a wellness slogan often skips that basic job. It is not a separate detox organ. The liver and kidneys still do the heavy clearing work you can measure.

## Emotions and psychosomatics

Long sitting, tight clothing, and very little movement change how heavy legs or ankles feel by evening. That is fluid and posture as much as mood. Stress can still shrink how much you move or sleep, and both matter for comfort. Feeling puffy after a hard week is a reason to walk and to rest. It is not proof that you need a special cleanse.

## Aromatherapy

No practical recipe belongs here. Topical oils do not open lymph vessels. Aggressive “detox” massage over nodes can hurt when there is infection or unknown swelling. If a scent helps you keep a gentle walk or stretch habit, treat it as a cue for movement, not as drainage. Stop any oil that irritates skin over nodes or scars.

## Daily care

- Move through the day: walks, shoulder rolls, anything that changes posture often.
- Sleep enough; dehydration and alcohol make evening puffiness more noticeable.
- Elevate tired legs when it helps, and wear comfortable clothes — not tight bands all day.
- Skip extreme fasts, colon “cleanses,” and gadget promises about lymph.
- See a clinician for persistent swelling, painful nodes, or fever with redness.

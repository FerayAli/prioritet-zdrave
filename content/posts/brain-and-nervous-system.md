## How does the system work?

The nervous system is how you sense, decide, and recover. The brain and spinal cord sit in the middle. Nerves carry signals to muscles and organs and back from skin, joints, and gut. A second branch shifts you between effort and recovery: heart rate, breathing, and digestion change with it even when you are not choosing them.

This is a map for everyday care, not a diagnosis of anxiety, migraine, or nerve disease. Sudden weakness, a severe new headache, confusion, or trouble speaking needs urgent care.

## Emotions and psychosomatics

Worry, grief, and long pressure change this system in ordinary ways: tighter muscles, faster breathing, a mind that will not shut off at night. That is physiology, not a verdict that the feeling is “only in your head.” Pain and fatigue also travel the other direction — a bad night makes the next day’s emotions louder. Naming the feeling can help you pick a habit (a walk, an earlier stop to the day). It does not replace care when mood or panic is running the week.

## Aromatherapy

Lavender is the scent most often studied around anxiety scores and sleep questionnaires. Rosemary aroma has been tested in short memory tasks, not as a memory treatment. Melissa and clary sage have small trials around calm. Read the limits in [lavender and anxiety](/blog/lavender-anxiety-what-reviews-say), [rosemary and cognition](/blog/rosemary-cognition-what-we-know), [melissa](/blog/melissa-lemon-balm-calm), and [clary sage](/blog/clary-sage-stress-research).

**Evening wind-down** — inhalation

- Lavender — 2 drops
- On a tissue for a few minutes, or in a diffuser in a ventilated room for about 20 minutes
- Do not swallow. Do not use undiluted on skin. Stop if the scent bothers you.

## Daily care

- Morning daylight, even a short walk, anchors the clock that later makes sleep easier.
- Leave a real pause between demanding blocks of work.
- Keep caffeine earlier if winding down is already hard.
- Eat regularly enough that a long gap is not pretending to be “stress.”
- If low mood, panic, or pain is the pattern of the week, talk with a clinician.

## Why this matters

Ginger is famous for **motion sickness and pregnancy nausea** research—but those studies overwhelmingly use **dried root, capsules, or teas**, not bottled essential oil.

## What researchers did

Meta-analyses pool RCTs of **oral ginger** for nausea in pregnancy, chemotherapy, and postoperative settings. Ginger essential oil appears in aromatherapy nausea studies far less frequently and with different dosing.

## Main findings

- Oral ginger at studied doses shows **reduced nausea severity** versus placebo in several pooled analyses for pregnancy and surgical contexts (with physician oversight in pregnancy).
- Ginger essential oil inhalation trials are **sparse** and not dose-equivalent to root preparations.
- Essential oil ingestion carries **mucosal irritation** risk and is not a substitute for studied root products.

## What this does not prove

Adding ginger oil to water or food is not supported by the nausea meta-analyses. Pediatric and pregnancy use require professional advice.

## Practical context

[Ginger](/book/ginger) in our book is a warming blend note; for digestion education we point to food and movement posts, not internal EO use.

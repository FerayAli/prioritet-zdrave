## Why this matters

Clary sage (*Salvia sclarea*) is marketed for relaxation and hormonal balance. Human research is **limited** and often tied to specific settings such as stressful work shifts or childbirth support—not general hormone therapy.

## What researchers did

Controlled studies apply clary sage essential oil via inhalation or massage in nurses, women in labor, or stressed volunteers, measuring **salivary cortisol**, blood pressure, heart rate, or anxiety scales compared with control aromas or usual care.

## Main findings

- Some occupational stress trials report **lower cortisol or improved relaxation scores** after clary sage inhalation sessions versus control.
- Maternity aromatherapy studies (often multi-oil protocols) describe **reduced pain or anxiety ratings**; clary sage is one component among several.
- Evidence quality is limited by **small samples, lack of blinding, and mixed oils**.

## What this does not prove

Clary sage does not regulate hormones in the endocrine sense based on current RCTs. Pregnancy and breastfeeding require clinician guidance before strong essential oil use.

## Practical context

[Clary sage](/book/clary-sage) is described on our site as a soft, herbal note—read studies for route and setting before copying protocols.

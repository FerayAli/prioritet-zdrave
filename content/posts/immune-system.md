## How does the system work?

Immunity is a network, not a single organ. Skin and the linings of the gut and airways are barriers. Cells recognize unfamiliar proteins. Signals raise or quiet inflammation. Lymph nodes are meeting points along the way. You notice the system most when it is busy — a sore throat, a healing cut, a fever — and least when it is simply maintaining you.

“Boosting” it is not a meaningful everyday goal. Too quiet, and infections slip through. Too loud, and you get allergy or autoimmune disease. Fever, a wound that is spreading, or infections that keep returning need a clinician. Vaccination is outside the job of a scent.

## Emotions and psychosomatics

Long stress and very short sleep change how immune cells behave in research settings, and they change whether you rest when you are ill. That is a real link. It is not permission to treat a fever as a mood, or to blame someone for getting sick. Feeling run down is a reason to sleep and to get care if the illness is more than a short cold. It is not evidence that an oil failed or succeeded.

## Aromatherapy

No practical recipe belongs in this section. Lab papers sometimes show an oil changing an immune marker in a dish or an animal. That is not evidence that diffusing it prevents infection. The distinction is written in [what immunology reviews actually measure](/blog/immune-claims-and-reviews). A mild bedtime scent can make a rest routine easier to keep. That is an indirect courtesy, not an immune stimulant. Stop any diffused oil that tightens your chest.

## Daily care

- Give sleep a fixed window most nights.
- Eat food you can recognize: plants, protein, and enough of both.
- Keep moving through the week, and leave recovery in the plan if you train hard.
- Wash your hands, stay home when you are actually ill, and air the room.
- Skip supplement stacks and “immune” blends until a clinician has a reason for them.

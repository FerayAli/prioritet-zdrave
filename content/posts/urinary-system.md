## How does the system work?

Kidneys filter blood all day. They decide how much water, salt, and waste leave in urine, and they help with blood pressure and with hormones involved in red blood cells and bone. Ureters carry urine to the bladder, which stores it until you empty it. The everyday job is filter, store, release.

The kidneys are already the filter. A tea, a bath oil, or a “detox” does not make them more virtuous. Pain on urination, blood in the urine, fever with back or side pain, or being unable to pass urine needs medical care the same day.

## Emotions and psychosomatics

Stress can change how often you notice the bladder, and some people pass urine more often when they are anxious. That does not create or cure an infection, and it does not mean urgency is a character trait. Pain, burning, blood, or fever are not psychosomatic explanations to sit with. They are reasons to be examined. Shame about leaking or frequency often delays that visit; the visit is the useful step.

## Aromatherapy

A practical recipe is not appropriate for this system. Do not put essential oils in bath water and call it a urinary treatment. Do not take them by mouth for “kidney support.” Do not use a blend sold for UTIs. A light scent in the room is only a room scent. Oils are a common source of skin irritation when they are added to washes for this part of the body; water and mild habits are enough.

## Daily care

- Drink water through the day so urine is pale yellow most of the time, unless a clinician has set a fluid limit.
- Empty the bladder when you feel the urge instead of routinely waiting for hours.
- Keep the area clean with water. Skip douches and scented products.
- Some painkillers, used often, are hard on kidneys. That is a conversation with a pharmacist or clinician.
- If infections keep returning, ask for an evaluation rather than a new product.

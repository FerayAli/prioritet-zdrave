## Why this matters

“Peppermint oil” in PubMed may mean **oral enteric capsules** for the gut or **10% topical application** for headache. Conflating them leads to unsafe or ineffective home experiments.

## What researchers did

- **Irritable bowel syndrome (IBS):** Meta-analyses of RCTs using **enteric-coated peppermint oil capsules** (defined doses, often several times daily for weeks).
- **Tension-type headache:** RCTs applying **diluted peppermint oil** to the forehead and temples versus placebo gel.

## Main findings

- Peppermint oil capsules showed **greater improvement in global IBS symptoms** than placebo in pooled analyses, with mostly mild adverse events (e.g. heartburn).
- Topical peppermint trials reported **reductions in headache intensity** for some participants versus placebo, with short treatment windows.
- **Menthol** is the major active odor/cooling constituent; concentration and formulation drive both benefit and irritation.

## What this does not prove

Swallowing essential oil from a bottle is not the same as enteric-coated pharmacy products. Full-strength peppermint on the face can irritate eyes and skin. IBS diagnosis and headache red flags need a clinician.

## Practical context

Our blends mention [peppermint](/book/peppermint) as a familiar digestive and cooling note—inhalation and careful diluted topical use—not oral essential oil.

## How does the system work?

Digestion starts in the mouth and continues through the stomach, small intestine, and colon. The liver and pancreas add bile and enzymes. The gut has its own dense nerve network, which is why a rushed meal or a tense afternoon can change bloating and bowel rhythm even when the food did not change. Fiber and water give those muscles something to work with.

Blood in the stool, trouble swallowing, unintentional weight loss, vomiting that will not stop, or pain that wakes you at night needs a clinician.

## Emotions and psychosomatics

The gut notices alarm quickly: appetite disappears, or the bowel hurries. That does not mean the symptom is imaginary, and it does not mean stress is the whole explanation. Reflux, infection, inflammation, and food intolerance are still physical. A calmer meal can help ordinary discomfort. It should not be used to dismiss pain that is new, severe, or persistent.

## Aromatherapy

Enteric-coated peppermint oil capsules have trial data for irritable bowel symptoms. Ginger root in food has a separate nausea literature. Neither result means “swallow the essential oil.” Peppermint can also worsen reflux for some people. Read [peppermint routes](/blog/peppermint-headache-and-ibs) and [ginger root versus ginger oil](/blog/ginger-oil-vs-ginger-plant).

**After-cooking air** — inhalation

- Ginger — 1 drop
- Peppermint — 1 drop
- Diffuse in a ventilated kitchen for about 15 minutes after you eat, or skip peppermint if reflux is your pattern
- Do not swallow either oil. This is not the capsule used in IBS trials.

## Daily care

- Sit down for meals when you can, and slow the first few minutes.
- Include fiber most days — fruit, beans, oats, vegetables — and increase it gradually.
- Leave a couple of hours between a large dinner and lying down if reflux is a pattern.
- A short walk after eating helps some people with that overly full feeling.
- Notice one food that reliably bothers you before you cut out entire food groups.

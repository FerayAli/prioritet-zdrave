## Why this matters

Lavender is among the most studied essential oils for calm and sleep-related stress. Summaries of randomized trials help separate **consistent signals** from one-off positive studies.

## What researchers did

Multiple PRISMA-aligned systematic reviews and meta-analyses pooled randomized controlled trials (RCTs) of lavender delivered by **inhalation**, **topical massage**, or **oral standardized lavender oil preparations** (e.g. Silexan), compared with placebo or usual care, in people with anxiety or in anxiety-provoking settings.

## Main findings

- A 2019 meta-analysis reported **lower anxiety scores** with lavender inhalation versus controls (pooled effect in the moderate range across validated scales; thousands of participants in included trials).
- Aromatherapy-focused meta-analyses similarly found favorable anxiety effects for **inhaled lavender**, with heterogeneity across studies (different doses, durations, and populations).
- **Oral standardized lavender oil** at defined daily doses for several weeks showed reductions on clinician-rated anxiety scales in some trials—not the same product as undiluted essential oil for diffusion.
- Physiological measures (e.g. blood pressure) were **inconsistent** across reviews.

## What this does not prove

Retail lavender essential oil for a diffuser has not been standardized like pharmaceutical oral preparations. Massage trials mix **touch, time, and scent**. Reviews cannot replace personal medical care for generalized anxiety disorder or panic symptoms.

## Practical context

Research often tests **short inhalation sessions** or **diluted topical use** under supervision—not ingestion of bottle lavender oil. See our [lavender](/book/lavender) note for how we talk about evening wind-down in plain language.

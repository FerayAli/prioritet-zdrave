## Why this matters

Tea tree (*Melaleuca alternifolia*) is one of the best-documented oils for **topical** antimicrobial activity in lab and some clinical skin studies—along with documented **allergic and irritant** reactions when misused.

## What researchers did

Reviews summarize in vitro activity against bacteria and fungi, plus clinical work on acne, athlete’s foot, and wound contexts using **diluted** tea tree preparations. Concentration and formulation (gel, shampoo, solution) vary.

## Main findings

- Laboratory studies consistently show antimicrobial effects against common skin-related microbes at defined concentrations.
- Some RCTs report improvements in **mild to moderate acne** or tinea compared with placebo or standard care when using commercial tea tree products at studied strengths (often 5%).
- **Contact dermatitis** and sensitization rates appear in dermatology literature, especially with undiluted or repeated application.

## What this does not prove

Antimicrobial activity in a petri dish does not mean swallowing or diffusing tea tree treats systemic infection. Oral use is associated with toxicity. Pets (especially cats) can be harmed by inappropriate exposure.

## Practical context

We mention [tea tree](/book/tea-tree) in educational skin and home contexts only with dilution and patch-test culture—not as internal medicine.

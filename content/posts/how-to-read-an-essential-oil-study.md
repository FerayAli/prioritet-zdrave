## Why this matters

Headlines compress complex papers into “oil X cures Y.” A few questions help you decide whether a study supports **your** use of a retail bottle—or only a specific lab preparation.

## What researchers did

This note is not a trial; it summarizes how pharmacology reviews classify essential oil research: chemistry, in vitro assays, animal models, and human RCTs or systematic reviews.

## Main findings — questions to ask

1. **Study type:** Systematic review or large RCT > small unblinded aromatherapy study > in vitro IC~50~ > social media post.
2. **Which substance:** Whole essential oil, isolated constituent (e.g. linalool, cineole), or patented oral product?
3. **Route:** Inhaled, diluted topical, oral capsule, or cells in a dish? Routes are not interchangeable.
4. **Chemotype and batch:** Same Latin name can yield different dominant terpenes (see our [chemotype post](/blog/chemotype-why-same-plant-differs)).
5. **Population:** Healthy students, hospitalized patients, or animals?
6. **Outcomes:** Subjective scales versus hard clinical endpoints (hospitalization, diagnosis improvement).
7. **Funding and registration:** Pre-registered clinical trials reduce selective reporting risk.

## What this does not prove

No checklist replaces professional care. Even strong reviews show **heterogeneity**—some trials positive, others null.

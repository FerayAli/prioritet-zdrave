## Why this matters

Melissa officinalis (lemon balm) overlaps with “calming herb” traditions. Published trials use **standardized extracts, teas, or combined aromatherapy**—not always the same as melissa essential oil.

## What researchers did

Double-blind RCTs have tested lemon balm extract for **generalized anxiety symptoms**, sleep quality in menopause, and cognitive effects when combined with valerian. Aromatherapy studies are fewer and often multi-oil blends.

## Main findings

- Some trials report **greater reductions in anxiety ratings** with lemon balm extract versus placebo over several weeks.
- Combined valerian–lemon balm studies suggest **improved sleep quality scores** in defined populations.
- Essential oil-specific RCTs are limited; concentration and route differ from herbal teas.

## What this does not prove

Melissa essential oil is potent and can irritate skin; it is not proven interchangeable with capsule extracts. Sedation may interact with other medications.

## Practical context

[Melissa](/book/melissa) is a prized, gentle-sounding oil in perfumery—treat research headlines as product-specific.

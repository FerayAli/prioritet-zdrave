## How does the system work?

Glands release hormones into the blood. Thyroid hormone sets a rough pace for metabolism. Insulin and glucagon move between a meal and storage. Cortisol and adrenaline rise with effort and with stress. Sex hormones shape cycles, bone, and mood across a longer arc. They talk to the nervous system constantly: a short night is already a hormone story the next morning.

This page will not read your labs. Unexplained fatigue, a swelling in the neck, strong thirst with frequent urination, or a cycle that has changed sharply belongs with a clinician.

## Emotions and psychosomatics

Ongoing stress and poor sleep change appetite, energy, and how steady the day feels. People sometimes hear that as “your hormones are just emotions.” The fairer sentence is smaller: mood and sleep change the conditions hormones work in, and hormone problems can change mood. Neither sentence diagnoses you. A scent does not balance estrogen, thyroid hormone, or insulin.

## Aromatherapy

A recipe that claims to regulate hormones is not appropriate here. Lavender can still mark a bedtime, which matters because sleep is one of the wider everyday inputs to this system. That is a cue, not endocrine therapy. The inhalation limits are in [lavender and sleep](/blog/lavender-sleep-inhalation-evidence). If a label promises to balance hormones, put it down.

## Daily care

- Eat at roughly predictable times, with protein and fiber on the plate.
- Walk after a meal when you can. Muscle contraction helps the body put glucose away.
- Protect one block of night sleep. Very late screens fight the same signals you are trying to steady.
- If you already take hormone medicine or monitor blood sugar, change food or training with the person who prescribed it.

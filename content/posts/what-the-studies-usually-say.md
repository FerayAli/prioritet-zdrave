## Why this matters

If you are new to research-backed essential oil reading, start with how we write science posts: cautious language, primary links, and clear limits.

## What we cover

Our science format posts summarize **peer-reviewed** work on oils in the [book](/book/lavender): anxiety and sleep (lavender), digestion routes (peppermint), respiratory context (eucalyptus), lab antimicrobial data (oregano, tea tree), and cross-cutting topics such as [chemotypes](/blog/chemotype-why-same-plant-differs) and [how to read a study](/blog/how-to-read-an-essential-oil-study).

## Main findings — habits that keep you honest

- Prefer **PubMed, PMC, or DOI** links over social media summaries.
- Match **route and product** in the paper to what you actually use.
- Treat **in vitro** results as early science, not bedside proof.
- Keep medical questions with a **clinician**.

## What this does not prove

A library of science notes does not make any single oil a treatment for disease. Individual posts state their own limits.

## How does the system work?

Sensory here means sight, hearing, balance, and the body’s map of touch and position. Eyes turn light into signals; the inner ear helps you stay upright; skin and joints tell you where you are in space. Most of the work is quiet until something fails — blur, ringing, dizziness, or numbness that does not pass. Sudden vision loss, sudden hearing loss, vertigo with weakness, or new numbness on one side needs urgent assessment. This map is not an eye-drop or ear-oil protocol.

## Emotions and psychosomatics

Stress tightens the neck and jaw, which can worsen headaches and make tinnitus feel louder in quiet rooms. Poor sleep blurs attention before it blurs the eyes themselves. That is real, and smaller than disease. Feeling “off balance” after a panic spike is common; so is true inner-ear illness. If dizziness repeats or comes with hearing change, see a clinician instead of guessing.

## Aromatherapy

No oil treats glaucoma, macular change, or hearing loss. Lavender inhalation is one of the better-studied bedtime cues: [the sleep reviews](/blog/lavender-sleep-inhalation-evidence). Use it only as a wind-down signal in a ventilated room — not near eyes, not in ears, not as drops.

**Bedtime cue** — inhalation only.

- Lavender — 2 drops on a tissue for a few minutes, or diffused about 20 minutes
- Do not put essential oils in the ear canal or on the eyelids.

## Daily care

- Get regular vision checks on the schedule your clinician suggests; update glasses when needed.
- Use hearing protection in loud settings; turn volume down on headphones.
- Move daily; balance improves with safe practice (tai chi, single-leg stands by a counter) when a clinician clears you.
- Take screen breaks; the 20-20-20 habit is boring and still helps eye comfort.
- Report new floaters with flashes, curtain-like vision loss, or one-sided hearing change promptly.

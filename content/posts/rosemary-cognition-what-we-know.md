## Why this matters

“Rosemary for memory” is a popular claim. Human data mostly cover **short-term** performance and mood after inhaling rosemary essential oil, not disease modification.

## What researchers did

Randomized crossover and parallel studies expose adults to rosemary essential oil via diffusion at controlled concentrations, then measure **cognitive task speed**, subjective alertness, and sometimes salivary stress markers versus control odors or no aroma.

## Main findings

- Some trials report **faster or more accurate memory tasks** and higher alertness ratings after rosemary inhalation at moderate concentrations.
- Higher concentrations in at least one study correlated with **slower memory performance**, suggesting a dose-response curve—not “more is better.”
- Mechanistic proposals involve **1,8-cineole** and cholinergic pathways; evidence in humans remains preliminary.

## What this does not prove

Rosemary diffusion does not treat dementia or ADHD. Results in young adults may not apply to older adults with cognitive disorders. Epilepsy and pregnancy warrant extra caution with strong aromas.

## Practical context

[Rosemary](/book/rosemary) appears in morning-focus blends on the site as a scent cue, not a cognitive drug.

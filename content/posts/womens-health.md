## How does the system work?

On this map, women’s health means the everyday conditions around a menstrual cycle, pelvic comfort, and the years when cycles change. Ovaries and the uterus are part of the endocrine story. Pelvic floor muscles support bladder, bowel, and sexual comfort. Breasts change across a cycle and across life. Sleep, stress, and hard training show up in all of that.

This is not a fertility plan, a pregnancy protocol, or a way to read hormones from symptoms. New pelvic pain, bleeding between periods, bleeding after menopause, a breast change, or pain that stops you walking should be assessed. During pregnancy, many essential oils are avoided. This site does not give a pregnancy blend.

## Emotions and psychosomatics

Cycle symptoms, pain, and hot flashes (when those are part of your life) are harder on short nights and in high-stress weeks. Mood changes across a cycle are common and still worth taking seriously when they flatten a life. “It’s just hormones” and “it’s just stress” are both too small. Pain that knocks you out of your day is a medical symptom. A calmer evening can sit beside that care. It does not explain endometriosis, heavy bleeding, or a missed period away.

## Aromatherapy

Clary sage is sometimes sold as a hormone oil. The human trials are small and mostly about stress markers in a session, not about fixing a cycle. Read [what those trials measured](/blog/clary-sage-stress-research). Lavender inhalation is one of the better-studied bedtime cues: [the sleep reviews](/blog/lavender-sleep-inhalation-evidence). Neither oil regulates estrogen, treats endometriosis, or replaces contraception or HRT.

**Bedtime cue** — inhalation. Not for pregnancy.

- Lavender — 2 drops
- On a tissue for a few minutes, or diffused in a ventilated room for about 20 minutes
- Do not swallow. Do not use this as a hormone blend. Do not experiment with clary sage, or with any oil, as a pregnancy recipe.

## Daily care

- Keep a simple note of cycle length, heavy days, and pain for two or three months if you are about to see a clinician.
- Eat enough. If periods are heavy, iron-containing foods are reasonable; only a blood test says whether you need a supplement.
- Walk, and keep some strength. Pelvic floor work is worth learning from a physio if leaking or pressure is the issue.
- Alcohol worsens sleep and, for many people, the next day’s mood and breast tenderness.
- Treat severe pain as a reason for care, not as something you should scent away.
